On val_37678, after listening to it. The two یک → یه errors are not the same error. In the first the speaker really does say یه and the annotation writes یک, so the model transcribed what was said and the scorer counted it wrong; the dataset is not consistent here, and of the 200 rows analysed for Task 4, eight references write یک and seven write یه. In the second the speaker does say یک and the model got it wrong. Three of the four errors on this clip are the model's, not the annotation's.
Why this clip and not the others. It is the fastest speech in the set by a wide margin — 14.3 characters per second against a validation-split median of 9.3, which puts it at the 99th percentile. Quietness, the other obvious suspect, does not separate it: its RMS is −28.1 dBFS while val_16322, transcribed perfectly, is quieter still at −29.0. Speaking rate is what distinguishes this clip, and it is the one the model struggled with.

A second thing falls out of the same measurements. val_31021 peaks at exactly 0.0 dBFS — it is clipped, one of the 22.10% Task 1 found and left untreated — and it is one of the two clips transcribed perfectly. One clip proves nothing on its own, but it points the same way as the arithmetic in Task 3: clipping is not what drives the remaining errors.
